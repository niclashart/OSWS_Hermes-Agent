# Right Diagnosis, Unsafe Route: Auditing Clinical AI Decision Trajectories and Corrective Oversight for Validity and Potential Harm

arXiv: 2611.22612 · screened 2026-11-20 · score 7/10

## Core idea
Shifts stepwise/agent evaluation from "did the system reach the right final answer" to "were the intermediate decisions safe". Two audits: a **trajectory audit** replays a case checkpoint by checkpoint (evidence released over time) and rates every intermediate decision (test, treatment, disposition) for potential patient harm and clinical validity, using an LLM judge calibrated by a physician reference standard (triple annotation + senior adjudication); an **oversight audit** branches at the first actionable error and measures whether corrective feedback repairs the error and whether it introduces new harm.

## Key results (verbatim quotes)
- "Across 300 cases from a hospital cardiology cohort, MIMIC-IV and the public ASCENT benchmark ... 55.2% of hospital trajectories reaching an acceptable final diagnosis passed through a decision with moderate or severe potential harm and 24.8% through a clinically unacceptable one; the harm rates were 76.2% on MIMIC-IV and 39.6% on ASCENT." (Abstract)
- "Structured corrections resolved 73.6% of first errors versus 24.8% after bare rejection, yet new potentially unsafe decisions followed 30.2% of corrections versus 19.7% of rejections" (Abstract; Sec. 5 reports 79.x% for a subset and the same 30.2%/19.x contrast)
- "LLM judge and clinicians diverge most." on the severity of harm; "71% of the judge's severe calls land at a lower level" in judge-vs-physician calibration (Sec. 5, Figure 7 discussion).
