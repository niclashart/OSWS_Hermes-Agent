# Signed but Steerable: Measuring and Closing the Text-Vector Integrity Gap in Offline RAG (arxiv-2611.06498)

**Score:** 8/10 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06498)

## Core idea
Signed knowledge packs for offline/edge RAG prove who published the pack, but not that its precomputed embedding vectors actually encode the signed text (text-vector integrity gap). The paper derives the "margin law": the fraction of queries an attacker who controls vectors can hijack equals the distribution of angular retrieval margins at the verifier's cosine tolerance. It proposes exact verification of quantisation codes with a calibrated guard band and canonicalisation, which accepts all honest vectors while leaving the publisher no attack freedom.

## Key results
- > "the hijack rate equals the distribution of angular retrieval margins at the tolerance (the margin law)" (Sec. 3)
- > "on a Raspberry Pi 5 running a local language model, such an attack lowers correct answers from 0.75 to 0.39" (Abstract / Sec. 5.5)
- > "tampered vectors that all pass the audit put the attacker's document first for 25% of targets" (FAISS product-quantisation audit, Sec. 5.2)
- > "Verifying quantisation codes instead ... accepted every honest vector in our samples on three platforms and left the publisher no choice" (Abstract / Sec. 5.3)
